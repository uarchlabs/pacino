## Claude.ai Assessment

BP-131 ran cleanly. The regression passes 261 of 261 at both ends, the extra counters didn’t change any program’s figures, and the tree is unchanged.

I read the whole results file except the two full regression listings, lines 497 to 853 and 1515 to 1835.

Blocks with more branches than slots

Two slots: rare in these programs. Only 9 blocks, all in twocond (two conditionals and a jump). The FTB never had to throw out a stored conditional in any program, because no region in the test programs has three conditionals. So the split would hardly change anything at two slots here. The programs simply don’t test that case.
One slot: 243 such blocks across 10 programs. In 312 cases a branch’s resolution trained TAGE, SC, the loop predictor or ITTAGE using another branch’s saved prediction data. That is the wrong training behind TD#179: indir 97, recur 62, twocond 49, hist 34.
Mispredict cost: measured at 11.1 to 11.3 cycles on average (maximum 20) in the 21 ordinary programs, inside my 8 to 12 estimate. Programs with page walks average 90 cycles, but that’s the walk, not the predictor.

The bigger problem the counts show: one block end shared by every start in a region

73 of 260 FTB entries are used by more than one block start, up to 6.
92% of block-end changes are made by a different start from the last one to write it; the starts keep overwriting each other. deep shows 270 of 270, nest 115, region 78.
608 of 1521 jump updates replaced a different jump in the same field: two jumps in one region taking turns (deep 255 of 288).
In every over-capacity block where another start’s end was recorded, the end a split would write differs from it: 228 of 228 at one slot. With today’s entry format, building the split would add more overwriting of that shared value.

This is the region-sharing problem behind TD#168, and it affects both slot counts. The split doesn’t fix it, and on its own it would make it worse.

Mispredicts on a branch no slot held: most happen with room in the entry: 423 of 429 at two slots, 391 of 537 at one. The branch simply wasn’t in the entry, or wasn’t visible from that start. The IA didn’t trace why, program by program.

Resolution against commit training

Wrong-path training: couldn’t be measured. tb_fe_top’s backend only resolves instructions that retire, in program order, so it never produces a wrong-path resolution. Measuring it needs an out-of-order backend model.
Resolve to commit: 6.4 cycles on average at two slots (maximum 24) in this in-order testbench. Even so, 55% of branches had their FTB region read again before their entry committed, and 876 of those reads saw an entry the resolution had already changed. Training at commit would have shown those reads older data. A real core’s gap would be longer.

Two defects found along the way

The uBTB can store one branch in both fields. This happened 82 times at two slots (hist 54, twocond 15, multistart 13). The uBTB fills whichever field the update names and never checks the other field.
The BP-119 placement rule can push out a later branch while a slot is free. This happened 31 times at two slots. The rule picks the lowest slot that is free or holds a branch at or after the new one’s position.

Decisions for you

Whether the region-sharing problem (TD#168) comes before the block split.
Whether to open TDs for the two defects.
Whether wrong-path training is worth an out-of-order backend model in tb_fe_top.

## Background
Each fact below was read from a file; check it against the tree.

- ftb_decisions.md section 3 says a block with a third conditional
  ENDS at the second conditional and the third starts the next block.
  That is not built at either slot count. ftb_cntrl.sv maps a
  conditional update to a field in this order: a field at the
  update's region position (the same branch); else a free field, br0
  first; else the field the update's port slot names; else the field
  hidden from this start. So a new branch arriving when both fields
  hold other branches REPLACES one of them. The block end written is
  ftb_upd_pft_addr_u0 whatever happened to the fields.
- ftq_resolve.sv sets that block end to the resolved end of a taken
  jump, else to the entry's PREDICTED fall-through. A resolution whose
  position names no slot of its entry is PLACED (BP-119 rule: the
  lowest slot free or holding a position at or after it, else the
  last slot) and trains every predictor from that slot's metadata.
- ubtb.sv fills the conditional field the update's br_idx names and
  reports the jump in the lowest slot carrying no visible
  conditional. At NUM_PRED_SLOTS = 1 only slot 0 exists, so a block's
  second branch, conditional or jump, is never reported (BP-128 D2).
  At 2, a jump behind two visible conditionals is not reported.
- ftb_cntrl.sv hides a conditional stored past a visible jump, and an
  FTB entry is shared by every block start in its 32-byte region
  (ftb_decisions.md 4.6): the block end (pftAddr) is one value for all
  of them, holding whichever start wrote it last (TD#168).
- ftq_resolve.sv drops a resolution whose entry is outside the live
  window, but cannot detect one whose entry index was reallocated (its
  header: no generation travels with the instruction). Training
  happens at resolution, not at commit (ftb_decisions.md 5).
- BP-128 to BP-130 figures at 2 and 1 slots (cycles, mispredicts):
  twocond 533 / 11 and 952 / 36; indir 1164 / 24 and 1668 / 71; hist
  871 / 22 and 1057 / 42; loops 453 / 14 and 536 / 23; multistart
  412 / 7 and 647 / 19; rvc 586 / 9 and 747 / 18. BP-129 counted 72
  backend mispredicts at 1 slot of a branch held in no slot.

This is structural, not a property of the RTL. The tb_fe_top backend
resolves a control transfer only after it retires: when the next
expected instruction retires, or when it detects the mispredict at
decode; it never resolves an instruction it did not retire, and it
presents resolutions in program order from one queue on port 0. So no
resolution is for a wrong-path instruction, none precedes an older
redirect, and an entry is never squashed under a queued resolution
unless a trap or replay redirects with _self set at the same entry
(the zero drop and realloc counts show it did not happen here). The
live-window drop and the reallocation case are reachable in principle
through that trap path only. Measuring wrong-path training needs a
backend model that executes and resolves out of order, including
wrong-path branches; this testbench has none.

## Decisions made not explicit in the prompt
All provisional under decision 4 (session-075 ruling, item 4): Jeff was
not present (automated mode), so the IA's recommended option was used.
1. Executed block (P1(a)) = the FTQ entry as executed: consecutive
   retired instructions with one FTQ index and no backend redirect
   between them. Not chosen: an ideal block from the start to the first
   taken branch, bounded by FTB_BLOCK_BYTES, independent of what the
   predictor did. The chosen form is what the hardware built; it differs
   between 2 and 1 slots (2813 and 2854 blocks).
2. P1(d) counts p2 groups the FTQ accepted (u_ftq.w_ok_slot_p2), not
   every FTB answer, so squashed p2 groups are not counted.
3. P2(a) is taken at the resolution with mispredict set, not at the
   redirect port. They are one to one in this testbench.
4. P2(c) is measured from the FTQ taking the redirect to decode's output;
   the testbench's detection delay is reported beside it, not added.
5. P3: a start "hits" an entry on any granted FTB lookup that hits,
   wrong-path lookups included; the end a start "currently needs" is the
   pftAddr its own last FTB update wrote since the entry's allocation;
   entry identity is the region from one allocation to the next.
6. P3 split end = the PC plus length of the NUM_PRED_SLOTS-th branch on
   the executed path (section 3: the block ends at that branch).
7. P4 "reallocated" is detected with a per-index allocation count
   (u_ftq.w_ok_pred_p1 at bpu_pred_idx_p1), compared between the time the
   resolution was queued and its acceptance.
8. P5 "predictor entry read again" = a lookup of the same 32-byte region
   in the FTB (granted, hit or miss) or the uBTB. The TAGE, SC, LP and
   ITTAGE entries were not tracked: their entry identity is per
   provider table and history, and the metadata needed to match a later
   read to the trained entry was not instrumented this session.

## Other Notes
Things the counts show that the questions did not ask:
- The FTB update stream and its case split are identical at 2 and 1
  slots, and so are all P4 counts: NUM_PRED_SLOTS changes what the slots
  report, not what the FTB stores. Every slot-count difference in these
  programs comes from reporting and from the uBTB.
- FTB conditional-field replacement (cases 3 and 4) is never exercised
  by tb_fe_top. A program with three or more conditionals in one region
  is needed to observe the behaviour the Background describes.
- uBTB duplicates at 2 slots (82): one branch held in both conditional
  fields, because the uBTB fills the field the update's slot names.
- At 2 slots 31 BP-119 placements went into an occupied slot although
  the entry had a free one (plcOcc 54 minus plcFull 23): the placement
  rule takes the lowest slot that is free OR holds a position at or
  after the branch, so it can displace a later branch while a slot is
  free.
- Mispredicts at a position no slot held with the entry not full are
  the larger group at both slot counts (423 at 2, 391 at 1).
- Group C mispredict costs (mean 90, max 218 cycles) are dominated by
  the page walk or fault on the target, not by the predictor.
- The hist det maximum at 1 slot (14) is one case where the instruction
  after the mispredicted branch reached decode 14 cycles after the
  branch; all other det values are 6 or less.
