The bug

TAGE and ITTAGE accept predictions and updates during their SRAM init walk, and nothing upstream holds requests until the walk finishes. In FD-1:

ITTAGE answers predictions from cycle 2, but its walk ends at cycle 513.
TAGE answers from cycle 1088 and accepts updates from cycle 1025, but its walk ends at cycle 2049.

tage_interfaces.md and ittage_interfaces.md both say no prediction or update happens before ready. SC follows that rule and has a unit test for it (TC2). TAGE and ITTAGE compute their ready and only drive it out. In fe_top, all three readies end on unread wires.

Why nothing caught it. Every unit test of TAGE and ITTAGE waits for ready before sending traffic, so the gate’s absence never shows. Every fe_top test runs with fast init, which makes the readies true at time 0. FD-1 is the first integration run of the real walk.

Effect.

Correctness: nothing wrong reaches retire. Predictions only steer fetch, and wrong ones are corrected at p2, p3 or by the backend; FD-1’s stream retired correctly.
Accuracy: lost during the walk.
Silicon: reads of uninitialized RAM there could produce spurious hits and targets.
Updates during the walk: they race the init writes.

All of these breach the documented rule.

Fix options, as the IA set out:

Gate inside TAGE and ITTAGE, as SC does. Requests before ready are ignored and fetch runs on the uBTB and FTB alone during the walk.
An upstream hold in ftq_npc on the three readies. Fetch waits about 2048 cycles after reset.
Both.

I recommend option 1. It’s what the interface documents specify, it matches SC, and fetch doesn’t stall at reset. FD-1, what it shows instead (DEMO FAIL kept, as FD-0c and Problem 3
direct): the three walks run and their readies rise (ITTAGE 513, SC
1025, TAGE 2049; each seen low first; RAS ready at 1). The FTQ requests
at p0 from cycle 0 and accepts a p1 prediction at cycle 1. ITTAGE
answers p2 predictions from cycle 2 (its walk ends at 513); TAGE
answers from 1088 and accepts updates from 1025 (its walk ends at
2049); SC answers from 1089 and updates from 1025, after its ready.
TAGE's start at 1088 follows SC's ready through tage consumer_ready,
not TAGE's own. bpu_tage_rdy, bpu_ittage_rdy and bpu_sc_ready are
"unread outputs" in fe_top.sv. tage_interfaces.md and
ittage_interfaces.md (RAM Init Interface) say no prediction or update
cycles begin before the ready, that the predictor ignores requests
before it, and that upstream logic must comprehend it; neither the
predictors nor the FTQ do. Design defect, not fixed (Constraints).

FD-13: no straight-line program gives a merge on this design. The IFU
reuses the previous request's line slot when a block's first line
equals the previous request's line (TD-IFU-8), so a second block on
the same line never reaches the L1I. The program instead trains a JAL
from the upper half of a line X back to X, then FENCE.I, then runs the
path again: the block at X+0x30 requests X and X+1 (IFU-7), and the
predicted block at X requests X while its miss is in flight. One merge.

FD-14 / FD-15: the V=0 to V=1 change rides on an ecall: be_trap_hook
applies set_v(1), S-mode, vsatp Sv39 and hgatp Sv39x4 as the ecall
retires (standing in for the trap to M and the return to VS). Tables
for both stages are written before reset.

## Assumptions made not explicit in the prompt

- "Retired instructions, cycles and mispredicts" are the figures of
  each program's line "<p>: N retired in C cycles, M mispredicts"; C
  stops at the last retirement (TD#167).
- The "proof" of FD-1, "the first prediction follows", means the
  first prediction the FTQ accepts is no earlier than the last ready.
- A demonstration run whose stream does not retire whole is a DEMO
  FAIL whatever its proof says.
- The bound properties are left out of the demo model: a
  demonstration checks no properties, and they would add build time.
- Monitors count only while the stream runs (meas); the 100-cycle
  drain, in which decode is no longer taken, would otherwise show
  backpressure in every scenario.
