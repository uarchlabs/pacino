## Background
TD#142. ftq_ifu_interfaces.md section 4: ftq_ifu_commit_ptr is
DRIVEN CONTINUOUSLY, not requested, carries no valid, and is not
part of the handshake. The FTQ already holds the pointer; what is
new is exporting it. It exists for uncached fetch alone: the IFU
compares an uncached block's index against it and issues the bus
transaction only when everything earlier has committed (IFU-22). A
cached fetch never reads it.

TD#141. ftq_backend_interfaces.md 5.1 U3: on RC_UNSPEC the FTQ
squashes EVERY entry, not entries after an index, and restarts
allocation at _pc. U1 and U2 make _idx, _pos and _self meaningless.
The flush index that expresses "every entry" is the oldest live
one, which is the commit pointer's index; section 5 drops every
in-flight fetch at or after the index it is given. Driving fetch_idx
instead leaves the fetches between commit_ptr and fetch_ptr
unflushed. READ 5.1 AND SECTION 5 AND SAY WHETHER YOU AGREE before
building it; if you read them differently, stop and report rather
than implementing either reading.

TD#140. ftq_entry_formats.md 4.3 R3a, ruled session-073: a
predecode redirect on K flushes at K and fetch restarts from K
(ftq_ifu_interfaces.md 7 W3), so K is fetched twice by design. Its
second writeback arrives with wb_rcvd set and gen[K] unchanged,
which 6.1 X3 accepts. It is accepted, section 7's W1 and W2 apply
to the fields, and it derives NO redirect. R3's bound -- one
predecode redirect per entry -- is unchanged and is what stops a
refetch loop.

## Assumptions made not explicit in the prompt
- The IFU's reading of "at or after the flush index" is measured from
  its own oldest in-flight entry. That is the only reference an IFU
  has; the tb_ftq H model uses it.
- Retiring an entry through commit implies its writeback returned.
  The model clears those bits. Section 7 says the FTQ does not need
  the writeback to free an entry, and the backend cannot retire
  instructions that were never fetched.
- "Tracks the pointer across allocation, commit and a redirect" in
  tb_ftq_ifu is met by driving commit_ptr as ftq_commit would, since
  ftq_ifu is combinational and owns no pointer. The same three
  events are also checked against the real pointer in tb_ftq H.
- "Not reported as an error" for the second writeback means, on the
  FTQ side, that it is accepted by the 6.1 test and sets status like
  any writeback. It is not dropped and not flagged. That is the
  acceptance half added to I4.
