## Background

ras_decisions.md 4.4 states that RAS restore on flush follows the
same pointer-restore protocol as mispredict recovery, from the FTQ
snapshot. That is D2 of ftq_backend_interfaces.md 5, which is
implemented in ras.sv and exercised in tb_ras. The backend redirect
group carries mispredict, trap and replay on ONE port set, so a trap
reaches the RAS through the same restore as any other redirect.

## Other Notes

- An architectural point was clarified during the discussion that
  this project appears to assume everywhere and state nowhere:
  EVERY BPU STRUCTURE IS SPECULATIVELY UPDATED. fe_decisions.md 7.1
  triggers updates at post-execute resolution and says they do not
  wait for retirement, so a younger branch can resolve, update the
  tables, and then be squashed by an older mispredict. The
  predictor tables are trained on wrong-path branches by design.
  The RAS is not special in being speculative; it is one of two
  structures whose state is a POINTER, and pointer corruption costs
  a guaranteed mispredict on every return until the window closes,
  where a polluted counter costs a fraction of one. That is why the
  entry carries a RAS snapshot and carries nothing for TAGE. Not
  written up as an FE rule; noted here because it is the reasoning
  behind 4.4 and it is nowhere in the registries.
- RAS corruption is self-correcting from BOTH ends: TOSR unwinds
  toward BOS and falls through to the commit stack when they meet
  (3.2), and BOS advances toward TOSR on every commit (3.3). This
  is now in 4.4 as supporting evidence that no flush-specific
  behaviour is needed.
- Written retrospectively. The Prompt section is a reconstruction,
  as in BP-103.
