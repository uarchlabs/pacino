  The part that changed my answer

  I said earlier this was "add two fields to bp_ftq_entry_t." That was wrong,
  and the reason matters.

  These are flops, outside both SRAMs — two vectors 64 deep, 128 bits total.
  Three reasons, and the third decides it:

  - The writeback arrives at an arbitrary time relative to the fetch read, so
    setting a bit inside the fast-path entry is a read-modify-write of an SRAM
    already read every cycle.
  - The commit walk and the next-PC hold test these across entries; a flop
    vector reads whole, an SRAM reads one index at a time.
  - A redirect rewind squashes every entry after an index and must clear their 
    status in one cycle. In flops that's a masked clear. In SRAM it's a walk —
    and the walk would have to finish before those indices could be reallocated.

  So the entry totals in §1 are untouched: fast path 224b, slow path 421b/slot,
  68,224b. The 128 bits sit outside both.

  allocation discriminates on any reallocation.

  second toggle restores the original value. That can't happen here only because
  §5 has the IFU discard everything for flushed entries, so the sole surviving
  stale writeback is one already presented in the flush cycle, arriving
  immediately. I recorded this explicitly, including the trigger to revisit: if 
  the IFU flush contract changes so a writeback can survive arbitrarily long 
  after a flush, the width must be revisited. That's the kind of assumption that

  The reason: RAS is one of only two speculatively-updated structures in the BPU

  Everything else is written only on the update channels, and those are

  resolves, so it never writes. TAGE, ITTAGE, SC, FTB and the uBTB are therefore
  never corrupted by a mispredict — there is nothing in them to repair, so they
  appear in redirect discussion not at all.

  Two structures are different, because they are written at predict time on the
  speculative path:

  - bp_history — the GHR/PHR pointers advance at p1 on every prediction. Hence
    ckpt_wr_en = r_val_p1, and TD-FE-7.
  - The RAS — TOSR/TOSW advance on every predicted call and return.

  checkpoint and bp_ras_snapshot_t. Not one per predictor — one per
  speculatively-updated structure.

  And this is the part that answers your objection directly

  A conditional branch mispredict corrupts the RAS. Not because the conditional
  touches it, but because the wrong-path blocks fetched behind that conditional
  may have contained calls and returns, and those pushed and popped the
  speculative stack. Squash the conditional and TOSR/TOSW are now wrong — for a
  structure the conditional has nothing to do with.

  So the RAS needs repair on every redirect, from every branch type. Its
  prominence isn't about returns at all; it's that it's one of two things a
  redirect has to put back.

  Plus a second, unrelated reason it keeps surfacing

  The RAS is the only predictor with a commit dimension — BOS advances at
  commit, restore > commit > hold. That makes ras_commit_val a scalar port with
  no slot dimension, which is why ftq_decisions.md §5.4 rate-limits the entire
  commit walk to one entry per cycle. As it says: the limit is the RAS, not the
  FTQ.

  commit throughput, flush — for two distinct structural reasons, neither of

  What the axis actually is

  - A polluted TAGE counter is off by one step on one entry. Expected cost is a
