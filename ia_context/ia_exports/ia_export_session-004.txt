  The three groups

  Fetch request (decoupled) — start_pc, next_pc, idx, taken_val, taken_pos.
  next_pc is the §2.4 successor, so the IFU knows where the block ends without
  reading the prediction. taken_pos truncates the bundle.

  Flush — one group, flush_val + flush_idx. Drop every in-flight fetch at or
  after that index.

  Predecode writeback — pd[16] of ftq_pd_info_t, plus range, cfi, mis, target,
  fault. No separate IFU→FTQ redirect port; the FTQ derives it, as XiangShan
  does.

  Three decisions worth your attention

  The writeback forms no predictor update. FE-8 keeps updates post-execute. A
  structural mispredict rewrites the entry; the predictors learn it when the
  branch resolves, and §4.2.1 R2 already handles a classification disagreeing
  with the resolved type. Forming an update at predecode would give the
  predictors two producers in different orders and break FE-6.

  One flush group, not XiangShan's two. They export s2 and s3 pointers and make
  the consumer apply shouldFlushByStage2/3. FE-3 says the FTQ has already
  resolved supersession by stage order. Exporting both would ask the IFU to redo
  that.

  The writeback is the third correction of a slot, after the p2 and p3 slot
  groups, superseding both by the same stage-order rule. W1-W4 in §7 define
  exactly what it rewrites, including the history and RAS restore.

  The RVA23 gap, §10

  This is the part I'd want you to read. RVA23 mandates C, so a branch can start
  at any 2-byte boundary. FTB_BR_POS_BITS = 3 is 4-byte granular.
  bp_history_interfaces.md:188-192 and ftb_decisions.md 4.4 both already accept
  that two RVC branches in an aligned word share a position — but neither states
  the consequence.

  Stated here: when both halves of a word hold a branch, the entry can describe
  only one, and the writeback's 4-bit position must drop its low bit to fit
  (W2), so the information is lost at the interface. The FTB and uBTB train the
  two as one branch and their directions interfere. Correctness holds — a wrong
  prediction is a redirect, not a fault — but the accuracy loss is real and
  nothing measures it. The fix is FTB_BR_POS_BITS = 4 plus wider FTB position
  and pftAddr fields, which is an FTB change, out of scope here.

  That mismatch is why the interface carries two position widths at all:
  FTQ_PD_POS_BITS = 4 for predecode, FTB_BR_POS_BITS = 3 for the predictor.

  Left open deliberately, §8

  The ICache path (XiangShan drives it from the FTQ for timing; I routed through
  the IFU and left ftq_icache undecided). Whether the IFU coalesces two 32-byte
  entries into one 64-byte access. And the three entry fields this interface 
  needs — request-issued, writeback-received, fault — which I did not add to 
  ftq_entry_formats.md, because the allocation and deallocation policy that
  would read them is still FE-U7. Those want deciding together, which points at
  ftq_backend next.

● It matters for three concrete reasons, and yes, there is one decision.

  Why it matters

  Position is stable; a slot id is not. Position is (branch PC − block base) / 4
  — a property of the instruction's address, fixed forever. The slot is
  predictor bookkeeping: which of the FTB's two branch fields the branch landed
  in. And the entry's slot contents get rewritten after fetch — by the p2 slot
  correction group, again at p3, and again by the predecode writeback. If the
  backend captured a slot id at fetch and returned it at resolution, that id
  could name a slot whose contents had since changed. Position cannot go stale.

  It bounds what every in-flight instruction carries. FTQ_IDX_BITS 6 +
  FTB_BR_POS_BITS 3 = 9 bits per instruction, from IFU through IBuffer, rename
  and ROB to retirement. A slot id would be a tenth bit that is derivable from
  the ninth.

  It keeps bp_update_t unchanged. That struct is shipped and verified. It is
  already arrayed [NUM_PRED_SLOTS-1:0] with the slot as the array index, so once
  the FTQ maps position to slot it writes the matching element. No change to a
  struct that tb_bp_pkg width-checks.

  The decision I need

  Confirm assumption A1: instructions carry (ftq_idx, pos) — 9 bits — from the 
  IFU through to retirement. Everything in §4 and §6 rests on it, and I could

  Three things I'd point you at

  §4.3 states the priority as a consequence, not an axiom. The rule that
  actually matters is that the correction naming the oldest entry wins, since
  everything younger is squashed by it. The fixed priority above is that order,
  because each source sits at a known pipeline depth: backend is behind fetch,
  predecode is behind p1, p3 lags p2. On a stall p2 and p3 hold the same entry

  The pointer model

  Three pointers, seven bits each — six index the array, the top bit is the wrap
  generation.

  alloc_ptr    head    advances when a p0 request is accepted
  fetch_ptr    middle  advances on the IFU handshake
  commit_ptr   tail    advances at most one entry per cycle

  FQ-1: commit_ptr <= fetch_ptr <= alloc_ptr. The gap between fetch_ptr and
  alloc_ptr is the predicted-but-unfetched run-ahead — at most 2 KiB of stream —
  and it is the decoupling the FTQ exists to provide.

  Four things worth your eye

  The index is committed at p0, not p1. ftq_pred_idx_p0 leaves with the request,
  so alloc_ptr must advance a cycle before the entry is written.
  fe_decisions.md §2.3 says the FTQ allocates at p1; that describes the entry 
  write. Both are now stated so they cannot be read as contradicting.

  Commit and free are one pointer, not two. I originally described a separate
  commit-walk pointer. They merge: the commit payload reads bp_ras_snapshot_t
  out of the entry, so an entry cannot be freed before its RAS commit is issued.
  One pointer does both, walking at one entry per cycle because ras_commit_* is
  scalar and FE-11 caps an entry at one RAS operation — so the walk exactly
  matches the port's capacity and can never fall behind.

  Stale responses use a shadow, not a wider index. The cluster's response
  indices carry no wrap bit, so a rewind can re-allocate an index while a
  response for its squashed use is in flight. The FTQ keeps a four-deep
  in-flight shadow, 28 bits, cleared on redirect. Both alternatives are recorded
  with their cost: widening the carried index would change FTQ_IDX_BITS at
  every cluster port and widen branch_id in four structs; draining the cluster
  before re-issuing would add up to three cycles to every redirect, more than
  doubling a p2 redirect.

  G23 falls out — the checkpoint is a field of the entry, reclaimed with it. No
  separate protocol.

  The finding that shaped it

  The FTB update is 14 flat ports with no slot dimension and no ready. There is
  no handshake protecting it. Presenting two updates in one cycle silently loses
  one — so this is a correctness requirement, not an optimisation, and it
  belongs to the FTQ because the FTQ is what has two channels.

  That reframing matters. G9 was filed as "arbitration," which reads like a
  fairness or throughput concern. It is neither.

  The rule

  S1  A channel is FTB-bound when its resolved br_type is anything
      but NO_BRANCH.
  S2  Both FTB-bound in one cycle: slot 0 goes, slot 1 holds in a
      one-deep skid, presented next cycle.
  S3  While the skid is occupied the FTQ accepts at most one
      FTB-bound resolution; two only when it is empty.

  Scope is the FTB alone. Every other predictor takes two per cycle — tage,
  ittage, sc and ubtb are all per-slot. The RAS port is scalar too, but it is
  fed from the §5.4 commit walk, not from resolution. That is why §5.7 sits
  beside §5.4: same class of problem, a scalar port with more than one source.

  Why slot 0 first is enough

  Two resolutions in the same cycle are simultaneous, so they have no relative 
  resolution order and FE-6 constrains nothing between them — any deterministic
  choice satisfies it. The skid preserves order rather than reordering, and the
  FTB has no queue to reorder anyway. And when both channels name the same
  entry, slot 0 first is program order, because IC-FTB-16 fills the conditional
  fields in program order.

  That is IC-FTB-16 paying off a second time, in a place I did not anticipate
  when you asked for it.

  Two things recorded rather than glossed

  Not mergeable. Two updates naming the same entry hit the same FTB way — the
  carried hit and way come from ftb_pred_meta_t, scalar within the entry — but
  ftb_upd_br_idx_u0 is one bit selecting br0 or br1. One write reaches one
  field. Two branches in one block need two writes.

  A known throughput ceiling. The FTB update path sustains one per cycle. Two
  FTB-bound branches resolving indefinitely would stall resolution. That is a
  throughput bound, not a correctness one, and nothing here can measure whether
  it is ever reached. Widening the FTB to a per-slot update port would remove it
  and would contradict FTB-3 / IC-FTB-09 — I did not reopen that choice.

  │ direction or target               │ wrong                               │
