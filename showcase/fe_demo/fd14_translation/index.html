    fd14_translation: Sv39 Walk, Sv39x4 Nested Walk and Svadu CAS¶

    Open waveform

    Runs Surfer in the browser, with nothing to install. The signal setup takes a few seconds to apply.

    1. Introduction¶

    fd14_translation (FD-14) demonstrates instruction address translation:

    1. an ITLB miss and an Sv39 page table walk
    2. a Svadu A-bit update by compare-and-swap
    3. a nested Sv39x4 walk, which translates through the VS-stage and the G-stage

    The program first executes in supervisor mode under single-stage Sv39 translation1, with hardware update of the PTE accessed (A) bit enabled. The leaf PTE of its code page has the A bit clear, so the page table walk sets the bit by compare-and-swap.

    An ecall then transfers execution to a guest under two-stage translation, and fetches in the guest walk both stages. Section 4 defines the RISC-V terms.

    The result line in the log file reports the walks by kind, the G-stage PTE evaluations and the compare-and-swaps.

    This scenario runs in the 1-prediction slot Pacino configuration, NUM_PRED_SLOTS=1.

    2. Run the scenario¶

    Run simulation: 

    make -C examples/fe_demo fd14_translation

    View the waveform (runs simulation first if FST is not up to date): 

    make -C examples/fe_demo view S=fd14_translation

    Console output: 

    -I: fd14_translation : PASS : cyc = 285

    Log file output: 

    -I: fd14_translation: PASS, single-stage walks 2, two-stage walks 2,
    G-stage page table entry evaluations 21, compare-and-swaps 1,
    events checked 13 of 13, cyc 285

    The testbench monitor, tb/fe_demo_mon.svh, supplies the counts in the log line, while the expected instruction stream has not fully retired. Walks are walker requests taken, and wrong-path walks are counted. The G-stage evaluations are one per PTE evaluated: 21 = 9 + 12, over the two walks with V=1.

    3. The program¶

    The program is written by the task prog_fd14 in programs/fd14_translation.svh. The code consists of a short prologue at the reset vector, a body on page C ending in an ecall, and a body on page V.

    The program executes in S-mode under satp Sv39 with ASID 1 and root 0x8F00_0000, with menvcfg.ADUE set. Page V is reached in VS-mode, under vsatp Sv39 with ASID 1 and hgatp Sv39x4 with VMID 3 and root 0x8E00_0000.

    The change of privilege is applied by the testbench. When the ecall retires, the testbench sets V=1, privilege S, vsatp and hgatp, in place of the trap to M-mode and the return to VS-mode that software would execute.2 The backend's trap redirect then fetches V.

    The c.addi and addi instructions do no useful work. Their purpose is to mix 16-bit and 32-bit instructions and to separate the control transfers.

    The run has no accepted p2 slot groups, because no block repeats and the FTB therefore never hits.

    4. Walkthrough¶

    The walkthrough follows the translation of each code page. Section 4.1 describes an ITLB miss and a single-stage walk, section 4.2 the Svadu A-bit update, and section 4.3 the nested walk.

    The RISC-V terms used below are as follows. Sv39 is the 3-level page table format with 39-bit virtual addresses, rooted at the CSR satp. Under the hypervisor extension, V=1 selects guest execution: VS-mode is the guest's supervisor mode, the VS-stage is the guest's translation, under the CSR vsatp, and the G-stage is the hypervisor's second-level translation, under the CSR hgatp. Sv39x4 is the G-stage format for Sv39 guests, with 41-bit guest physical addresses and a 16 KiB root table. Svadu is the extension under which hardware updates a PTE's accessed (A) and dirty (D) bits. menvcfg.ADUE enables it for single-stage and G-stage translation. Without it, a clear A bit raises a page fault for software to handle (Svade).

    Cycle numbers below are events in section 5; each event is a marker in the waveform. A signal the testbench drives, such as tb.mem_ptw_rsp_val, changes one time unit after the clock edge, so the waveform first shows the new value at the marker of the next cycle.

    The waveform group itlb contains the IFU lookups, walker the ITLB requests, tb.u_ptw.r_st and the responses, and walker_mem the walker's memory port.

    4.1 ITLB miss and single-stage walk¶

    The first fetch misses the ITLB at cyc 3 (event 1), and the ITLB requests a walk with V=0 at cyc 4 (event 2). The walker reads three PTEs from the root 0x8F00_0000, and the ITLB then answers the lookup with a hit.

    At cyc 3 (event 1), itlb_ifu_rsp_val is asserted with itlb_ifu_status 1 for ifu_itlb_vpn 0x80000. At cyc 4 (event 2), tb.itlb_l2t_req_val and tb.itlb_l2t_req_rdy are asserted with tb.itlb_l2t_vpn 0x80000 and tb.itlb_l2t_v 0. tb.ptw_mem_req_paddr is 0x8F00_0010 at cyc 7, 0x8F00_8000 at cyc 13 and 0x8F00_9000 at cyc 19. At cyc 25, tb.l2t_itlb_rsp_val is asserted with tb.l2t_itlb_status 0 and tb.l2t_itlb_ppn 0x80000. At cyc 26, itlb_ifu_status is 0 with itlb_ifu_ppn 0x80000.

    4.2 Svadu A-bit update¶

    The walk for page C starts at cyc 46 (event 3). The leaf PTE of page C is valid and permitted, with A clear. With ADUE set, the walker's leaf evaluation requests an update instead of raising a page fault.3 In a single-stage walk, the update issues one compare-and-swap, at cyc 67 (event 4), whose compare value is the PTE read and whose swap value is the PTE with A set.4 The walk answers at cyc 73 (event 5).

    At cyc 46 (event 3), the walk starts with tb.itlb_l2t_vpn 0x80010 and tb.itlb_l2t_v 0. At cyc 61, the walker reads the leaf at 0x8F00_9080. At cyc 67 (event 4), tb.ptw_mem_req_op is 1, with tb.ptw_mem_req_paddr 0x8F00_9080, tb.ptw_mem_req_cmp 0x2040_008B and tb.ptw_mem_req_swap 0x2040_00CB. The two values differ in bit 6, the A bit. At cyc 73 (event 5), tb.l2t_itlb_rsp_val is asserted with tb.l2t_itlb_status 0 and tb.l2t_itlb_ppn 0x81000.

    4.3 Nested walk¶

    With V=1 and hgatp not Bare, the walker enables the G-stage.5 Every VS-stage PTE address is a guest physical address, translated through the G-stage before the PTE is read, and the final guest physical address is translated the same way.

    The walk for page V, which starts at cyc 170 (event 9), therefore makes 15 memory reads: three VS-stage PTEs, each preceded by a 3-level G-stage translation of its address, and a 3-level G-stage translation of the final guest physical address. The VS-stage leaf is read at cyc 246 (event 10), and the walk answers at cyc 267 (event 11). The figure below shows the reads.

    The run has two walks with V=1. The first, at cyc 91 (event 6), is on the wrong path. The fetch has continued past the ecall, and when the ecall retires, a younger fetch on page C is translated with V=1. The backend's trap redirect at cyc 92 (event 7) flushes the block. Page C is not mapped in the VS-stage, so that walk ends with a fault at cyc 168 (event 8) that nothing delivers.6 This walk occupies the walker until then, so the walk for page V starts at cyc 170 (event 9). The first walk has 9 G-stage evaluations and the second has 12.

    At cyc 91 (event 6), tb.itlb_l2t_req_val and tb.itlb_l2t_req_rdy are asserted with tb.itlb_l2t_vpn 0x80010 and tb.itlb_l2t_v 1. At cyc 92 (event 7), tb.bkend_ftq_redir_val is asserted with tb.bkend_ftq_redir_pc 0x8005_0000. At cyc 168 (event 8), tb.l2t_itlb_rsp_val is asserted with tb.l2t_itlb_status 1 and tb.l2t_itlb_cause 12.

    At cyc 170 (event 9), the walk for page V starts with tb.itlb_l2t_vpn 0x80050 and tb.itlb_l2t_v 1, and tb.u_ptw.r_g_on is 1. At cyc 246 (event 10), tb.mem_ptw_rsp_val is asserted with tb.mem_ptw_rsp_data 0x0401_40CB: the VS-stage leaf, giving the guest physical address 0x1005_0000. At cyc 267 (event 11), tb.l2t_itlb_rsp_val is asserted with tb.l2t_itlb_status 0 and tb.l2t_itlb_ppn 0x81600.

    1. Sv39 is RISC-V's page-based virtual memory scheme with 39-bit virtual addresses. The fields of a virtual address are as shown:

      Bits Field Use
      38:30 VPN[2] index into the top-level page table
      29:21 VPN[1] index into the second-level table
      20:12 VPN[0] index into the third-level table
      11:0 offset byte within the 4 KiB page

      Each table holds 512 entries of 8 bytes. A walk reads up to three PTEs, starting from the root held in the CSR satp, and the final PTE gives the physical page number. A PTE can also be a leaf at a higher level, giving a 2 MiB or 1 GiB superpage. ↩
